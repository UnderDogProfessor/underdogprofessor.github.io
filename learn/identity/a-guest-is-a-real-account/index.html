A guest is a real account

Inviting an external collaborator creates an account in your directory that can hold groups and roles, authenticated by somebody else's controls.

Inviting an external collaborator creates an account in your directory. It is marked as a guest, it authenticates against their home organization, and in most other respects it behaves like an account you own.

That is the part worth holding onto. A guest can be added to groups. A guest can be granted roles. A guest reads what authenticated users can read, which in a directory is a great deal. The guest flag is a label, not a sandbox.

Authentication happening elsewhere is the genuinely useful part of the design and also the complication. You do not manage their password, their second factor, or the state of the machine they sign in from, unless your own policies say so explicitly. If their organization is compromised, an account in your directory becomes reachable with credentials you never issued and cannot rotate.

Guests also outlive the reason they were created. The project finishes, the invitation stays, and nothing generates a ticket to remove it, because no leaver process at your company covers somebody who never joined it.

List your guest accounts and sort by last sign-in. Everything that has not signed in for ninety days is a decision nobody has made yet.

CHECK YOURSELF

A guest account from a partner organization belongs to a group granting access to a document library. The partner suffers a credential breach. What is your exposure?

SHOW THE ANSWER

Whatever that group grants, reachable with credentials you did not issue and cannot reset

Why. The guest flag is a label on an account rather than a restriction on it, so group membership and the access it carries work exactly as they would for anyone else. There is no default read-only ceiling. Your directory will accept the sign-in because the partner's identity provider vouches for it, which is the whole arrangement. The uncomfortable part is the second half: the credential was issued elsewhere, so you cannot rotate it, and your only levers are removing the group membership or the account.
