A trust points in one direction

When one domain trusts another, the side doing the trusting is the side taking the risk, and a one-way trust still carries somebody else's compromise inward.

A trust is a statement about whose authentication you are willing to believe.

If your domain trusts a partner’s, your domain controllers will accept principals from theirs. Those principals can then be granted rights on your resources. The direction people picture and the direction consequence travels are not the same, and that is the part misread in design reviews.

Work it through. The trusting side is the side taking the risk. It has agreed to recognize accounts that somebody else creates, disables, resets and audits. Every assumption it makes about account lifecycle is now an assumption about another organization’s processes, made on their timetable and reviewed by their staff.

A one-way trust limits the direction. It does not limit the consequence. If they are compromised, every resource you granted to a principal of theirs is reachable with whatever that principal held.

Which is why the interesting question about a trust is never whether it exists. It is what was granted across it afterward, usually much later, often by somebody who did not know the trust was the reason their change worked.

Go and enumerate the trusts on your domain and note the direction of each. For the ones pointing inward, list what principals from the far side hold on your side. That list is the actual exposure.

CHECK YOURSELF

Your forest trusts a partner's forest, one way. The partner is compromised. What does that mean on your side?

SHOW THE ANSWER

Principals from the partner can be authenticated by your domain, so their compromise reaches whatever you granted them

Why. Trusting a domain means agreeing to believe the accounts it vouches for, which is why the trusting side is the side carrying the risk. Foreign principals can be granted rights on your resources, and usually have been, so every assumption you make about account lifecycle has quietly become an assumption about the other organization's joiner and leaver processes. The direction limits which way authentication flows, not how far the consequence travels. The useful question is not whether the trust exists but what was granted across it, often years after anybody reviewed it.
