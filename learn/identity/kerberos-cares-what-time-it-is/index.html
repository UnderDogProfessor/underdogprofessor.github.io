Kerberos cares what time it is

Kerberos rejects authentication when the client and domain controller clocks differ by more than the allowed skew, which is why a time problem presents as an access problem.

Kerberos timestamps its messages. That is how it stops somebody capturing an authentication exchange and replaying it tomorrow: the ticket says when it was made, and anything too old is refused.

For that to work, both ends have to agree on the time. The tolerance in a default Windows domain is five minutes.

Drift past that and authentication stops. Not degrades, stops. The ticket is rejected, and the message the user sees talks about credentials or access, because the layer reporting it does not know the real reason.

Which is why a time problem is one of the great misdiagnoses in Windows administration. Somebody spends an afternoon on group policy and permissions for a machine whose battery died and whose clock is now six minutes slow.

The structural version matters more than the trick. Domain members sync from the domain controller holding the PDC emulator role, and that one should sync from a reliable external source. When the chain breaks, machines drift apart quietly and nothing complains until they cross the threshold.

Two things worth knowing for your own estate: what your skew tolerance is set to, and what the PDC emulator is actually syncing from. The second answer is occasionally nothing at all.

CHECK YOURSELF

One server starts failing domain authentication while every other machine on the same subnet is fine. Nothing changed in Active Directory. What is worth checking before anything else?

SHOW THE ANSWER

Whether its clock has drifted beyond the allowed skew from the domain controller

Why. Kerberos puts timestamps inside its messages so a captured one cannot be replayed later, which means the two ends have to agree on the time within a tolerance, five minutes by default. Past that the ticket is rejected as out of date and the failure surfaces as an access problem rather than a time problem. The other three are real causes of exactly this symptom, which is why clock skew is worth eliminating first: it takes seconds to check and it is the one that leaves no trace in the directory.
