The PAC carries your groups

Kerberos tickets contain your group memberships, so a service builds your access from the ticket it was handed rather than from the directory.

When a domain controller issues you a ticket, it writes your group memberships inside it. That structure is the Privilege Attribute Certificate, the PAC, and it travels with the ticket rather than being looked up later.

A service that receives your ticket builds your access token from what the PAC says. It does not call the directory to ask what groups you are in. That is the point. It makes authorization fast and keeps domain controllers out of every file access in the organization.

It also means your group membership is a snapshot, taken at the moment the ticket was issued.

Add somebody to a group and they do not gain the access until they get a new ticket. Remove them and they keep it until the ticket they are holding expires. Ten hours is a common default. Signing out and back in is the usual advice because it forces a fresh ticket, which is the thing that actually updates the picture.

The PAC is signed with keys only domain controllers hold, so a service can tell a genuine one from an edited one. That signature is the entire trust model. If it could be forged, group membership would be whatever the ticket holder claimed it was.

Next time a permission change does not seem to take effect, check the age of the ticket before you check the permission. On Windows, klist will tell you when it was issued.

CHECK YOURSELF

You remove a user from a privileged group at 10am. At 11am they are still reading a file share that only that group should reach. What is the likeliest explanation?

SHOW THE ANSWER

They hold a ticket issued before the change, and the file server reads group membership from that ticket

Why. The server is not consulting the directory at all, so neither replication nor caching on its side is the mechanism. It reads the PAC inside the ticket it was handed, and that PAC was assembled when the ticket was issued, before you made the change. A second group is always worth ruling out, which is why that option is tempting, but the timing points at ticket age first. The fix is a new ticket, which is the real reason signing out and back in is the standard advice.
