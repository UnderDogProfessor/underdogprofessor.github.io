A volume spike can cost you events

Pipelines protect themselves when a source floods. The protection works, the platform stays up, and some of what happened never arrives.

Every pipeline has a defence against being overwhelmed. Rate limits, queue depth caps, sampling that engages under load, back-pressure that sheds what it cannot hold. They exist so one runaway source cannot take the platform down.

They work. That is the problem.

When the defence engages, the platform stays healthy and the dashboards stay green, because from the platform’s point of view nothing failed. Events were dropped on purpose. The system did what it was configured to do.

Two details make this worse than it sounds.

The capacity is usually shared, so the loss does not land on the source that caused it. A chatty application floods the ingest tier, and what goes missing is a slice of everything else arriving at the same moment.

And that window is exactly the kind of window an investigation cares about, because loud events and quiet ones happen at the same time.

So treat a volume incident as a coverage incident. Record the start, the end, and which sources were affected, and write it down where an investigator will find it later.

Find out what your pipeline does when a source exceeds its limit. Drops, queues, or samples are three different answers with three different blast radii.

CHECK YOURSELF

A misconfigured application floods your pipeline for two hours. Afterwards the platform is healthy and the dashboards look normal. What should you check before closing it out?

SHOW THE ANSWER

Whether other sources lost events during the same window

Why. Rate limits and queues are usually shared, so a flood from one source consumes capacity other sources needed, and the loss lands on them rather than on the noisy one. That makes this a detection gap across the estate for two hours, not a housekeeping problem with one application. The other three are reasonable follow-ups and none of them tells you whether that window can be trusted, which is the question an investigation will ask months later.
