An alert has to survive a shift change

Context that lives in one analyst's head evaporates at handover. Whatever was checked and ruled out has to be in the ticket or it did not happen.

An investigation produces two kinds of output. Only one of them tends to get written down.

The first is what was found. That goes in the ticket, because it is progress and because the next step depends on it.

The second is what was ruled out, and it usually stays in the analyst’s head. Checked whether the parent process was the software deployment tool. Confirmed the account belongs to a contractor who works nights. Looked at whether the same hash appears on other hosts and it does not. Each of those took time and none of them feels like a finding, so none of them is recorded.

Then the shift ends.

The next analyst opens a case with an hour of work behind it and no way to see that hour. They do the sensible thing, which is to start with the most likely explanations, which are the three already eliminated.

The fix costs a line each. Record what you checked and what it told you, even when it told you nothing. Especially then.

Look at a case your team handed over recently. Count the negative findings written in it. If the answer is zero, the handover cost more than it looked.

CHECK YOURSELF

An analyst investigates for an hour, rules out three explanations, and hands the case over at end of shift with the note still investigating. What has the next analyst lost?

SHOW THE ANSWER

The hour of elimination, so the same three explanations get checked again

Why. Negative findings are the expensive part of an investigation and the part least likely to be written down, because ruling something out feels like having made no progress. The evidence surviving in the case is exactly why this is easy to miss: everything the first analyst looked at is still there, and the conclusions drawn from it are not, so the second analyst re-derives them. Severity is normally recorded on the alert already, and most cases involve no reporting user at all.
