COLD CHAIN Lab 2: finding password guesses in the Windows Security log

A beginner lab where you play the guesser, type five wrong passwords at a lock screen, then find them as failed sign-ins in the Security log. The log can't tell you who was typing.

Level
1, Basics
Time
About 45 minutes
Lab computer
Windows 11 Enterprise Evaluation, build 26100, in VMware Workstation
Evidence
Security log, events 4625 and 4624

What this is

This is the second lab in COLD CHAIN, a set of hands-on incident response labs I’m building in public around a made-up company, Rimeport Cold Logistics. The setup is the same Windows 11 virtual machine from Lab 1.

The story: Dana comes back from lunch and her screen says her password was wrong several times, but she didn’t type anything. Did someone try to guess it?

What the lab asks

You save any wrong-password record you already have, then play the guesser: lock the screen, type five wrong passwords, and unlock as Dana. Write down the time of every step. Then find the failures in the Security log as event 4625, and read who was tried, why it failed and where it came from.

What I found

  • Five failures, spread over 4 minutes 39 seconds. The log shows them at 2:30:26, 2:30:31, 2:32:12, 2:34:17 and 2:35:05 PM, all for Dana, all Logon Type 2, with Sub Status 0xC000006A: a real account with the wrong password.
  • They came from the computer itself. Workstation Name WS-DSP-01 and Source Network Address 127.0.0.1. That fits: I typed them at the keyboard.
  • A typo and guessing look different in numbers. My accidental typo in Lab 1 was one failure, followed by a success 1 minute 10 seconds later. The guesses were five failures before a success.
  • Most sign-ins weren’t mine. While I was guessing, the log recorded six successful sign-ins for SYSTEM with Logon Type 5. Windows was starting its own background services.
  • The unlock looked like a sign-in. It was logged as Logon Type 2 again, the third time I’ve seen that on this computer.
  • Clearing the log left a mark. I cleared the Security log to start fresh, and the first record afterward is event 1102, “the audit log was cleared.”

What I missed, and what changed because of my run

My notes matched the log on times and counts, but I got things wrong, and I wrote them down in my notes so readers can learn from them.

  • I wrote that the log “proves my successful sign-in.” It doesn’t. It proves the account’s password was typed correctly, not who was typing.
  • My lock at 2:30 PM isn’t in the log at all. The only record of it is the time I wrote down.
  • I skipped a screenshot the lab asked for, and I wrote the wrong event ID once (4654 for 4624).

Running the lab also fixed the lab. It had said the guesses would take “about a minute,” so the answer now says times vary. It now explains the general Status line above the Sub Status, adds a screenshot prompt for the unlock record, and notes that clearing a log leaves event 1102.

Try it

The lab is on GitHub, and so are my notes and my exported .evtx log, so you can open the real records in Event Viewer. Do the lab before reading my notes. Your times will differ.
